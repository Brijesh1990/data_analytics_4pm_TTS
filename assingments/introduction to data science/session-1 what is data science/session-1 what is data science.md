# What Is Data Science?

## Definition

Data science is the field of using data, scientific methods, statistics, programming, and domain knowledge to extract useful insights, solve problems, and support better decisions.

It combines mathematics, statistics, computer science, and subject knowledge to turn raw data into information, knowledge, and actionable results.

## Main Characteristics of Data Science

1. **Data-driven**: It uses facts and evidence from data rather than relying only on opinions or assumptions.

2. **Interdisciplinary**: It combines statistics, mathematics, programming, computer science, and domain expertise.

3. **Problem-solving oriented**: It focuses on answering questions and solving real-world problems.

4. **Uses structured and unstructured data**: It can work with tables, numbers, text, images, audio, video, and sensor data.

5. **Involves data collection**: Data scientists gather data from databases, surveys, websites, applications, devices, and other sources.

6. **Requires data cleaning**: Missing values, duplicate records, incorrect formats, and errors must be handled before analysis.

7. **Uses exploratory data analysis**: Data is summarized and visualized to discover patterns, trends, relationships, and unusual values.

8. **Uses statistical analysis**: Statistical methods help measure uncertainty, test ideas, and identify meaningful relationships.

9. **Uses programming and computational tools**: Languages such as Python and R, together with databases and other tools, are commonly used.

10. **Uses data visualization**: Charts, graphs, dashboards, and maps make complex findings easier to understand.

11. **Uses machine learning**: Algorithms can learn from data to make predictions, classify objects, or identify patterns.

12. **Supports prediction and forecasting**: Historical data can be used to estimate future events or behavior.

13. **Produces actionable insights**: The goal is to help people make decisions or take useful action.

14. **Requires domain knowledge**: Understanding the business, scientific, or social context is important for interpreting results correctly.
15. **Handles large and fast-changing data**: Data science can process high-volume, high-velocity, and high-variety data.

16. **Uses experimentation**: A/B tests and other experiments can measure whether a change produces the desired result.

17. **Deals with uncertainty**: Results are often expressed as probabilities, estimates, confidence intervals, or expected outcomes.

18. **Requires communication**: Findings must be explained clearly to both technical and non-technical audiences.

19. **Is iterative**: Data scientists repeatedly refine questions, data, models, and conclusions as new information is discovered.

20. **Requires ethical responsibility**: Data must be collected and used fairly, securely, transparently, and with respect for privacy.

21. **Is reproducible**: Analysis should be documented so that other people can understand and verify the results.

22. **Creates deployable solutions**: Models and analyses may be integrated into applications, dashboards, business processes, or automated systems.

## Typical Data Science Process

1. Define the problem.
2. Collect the relevant data.
3. Clean and prepare the data.
4. Explore and visualize the data.
5. Analyze the data or build a model.
6. Evaluate the results.
7. Communicate the findings.
8. Deploy, monitor, and improve the solution.

## Simple Example

A company can use data science to predict which customers may stop using its service. It can collect customer activity data, clean it, find patterns, train a machine learning model, evaluate its accuracy, and use the results to offer helpful support to customers.

## Summary

Data science turns raw data into useful knowledge and decisions. Its most important characteristics are its data-driven and interdisciplinary nature, use of statistics and programming, ability to work with many types of data, focus on prediction and problem-solving, iterative workflow, clear communication, reproducibility, and ethical use of information.
